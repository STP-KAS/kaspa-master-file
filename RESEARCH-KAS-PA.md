## 2019 — before there was a chain

[1](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-site-feedback-category/1). The system account opens Site Feedback: talk about the site itself. No one did. The category is empty apart from this post.

[7](https://research.kas.pa/t/welcome-to-kaspa-research/7). The welcome, closed, two posts, 2,376 views. Kaspa Initiative is described as an open-source community building a cryptocurrency with Nakamoto-style robustness and EVM-style expressiveness. The forum is a call for researchers. ancapalex’s reply on 22 Sep 2019 is empty.

[23](https://research.kas.pa/t/coin-issuance-principles-and-thoughts/23). hashdag, 18 Sep 2019. Hash-pegged issuance: as hashrate grows, issuance goes down, and it never goes back up. There is a floor. The decrease per epoch is capped. He sketches a starting hashrate around 3,000 GPUs, a factor of 0.75, and a six-month epoch, and he credits Primecoin plus Alex Tran, Lulu Kiffer, and Ori Newman. He also says an extreme factor (his example is 1,000) collapses the idea into a pre-mine. This is not the 2021 fair-launch schedule. The live board’s supply rule is unchanged by this post.

[31](https://research.kas.pa/t/algorithmic-asic-mimicking/31). hashdag, 10 Oct 2019, five posts. A GPU proof of work that tries to copy an ASIC’s committed stake: a one-time upfront proof-of-work license proportional to a declared hashrate, then a short-window cap and a long-run cap. He states the drawbacks himself: the license links a miner’s blocks unless zero knowledge hides it, a liquid market in licenses can appear, and ASICs may still arrive. doronz talks about truthful declaration and range proofs. Michael points out that the license price depends on electricity, that a secondary market can centralize, and that a ramp (his example is 1/16 up to full) is softer than one license. Companion to topic 35. Not the launch.

[35](https://research.kas.pa/t/a-proposal-for-a-mechanism-to-launch-a-fair-healthy-and-successful-cryptocurrency/35). hashdag, 12 Nov 2019, one post, 1,243 views. He rejects a pre-mine, a Zcash-style founder reward, and Grin’s unfunded fair launch. The sketch is a universal cash investment (burn cash in proportion to hashrate), then a universal proof-of-work investment: one upfront proof of work. Early investors would pre-mine an amount of that proof equal to money already spent, hardcoded at genesis, and would still have to mine with GPUs after launch. This is not how Kaspa launched on 7 Nov 2021. The live row remains proof of work, fair launch, that date.

[37](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-economics-category/37) and [38](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-mining-category/38). ancapalex, 9 Dec 2019. Economics is for monetary policy, distribution, and fees. Mining is for mining. No further content in either about post.

## 2020 — the blockDAG arguments

[44](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-paper-review-category/44). ancapalex, 6 Jan 2020. Paper Review is for reviews of cryptocurrency research, with “more information to be added later.” No paper was ever posted in the category.

[50](https://research.kas.pa/t/the-twelve-minutes-problem/50). hashdag, 26 Feb 2020, eleven posts. A CPU-cost attack he calls the twelve minutes problem. The preferred proposal: validate blue blocks, and allow invalid reds as long as they are not in the blue set. He does not treat that as a consensus-security reorganization. Michael says a correct implementation was on the order of a second, not twelve minutes. someone235 says a deep reorganization should be slow and should not crash; a day at 1 block per second and width 10 was about 40 seconds in `restoreUTXO`. elichai2 times Bitcoin Core `invalidateblock` of 11,012 blocks at 20 minutes 34 seconds. The thread is the argument. It is not a shipped parameter.

[58](https://research.kas.pa/t/manipulating-difficulty-to-spam-the-utxo-set-is-ineffective/58). Deshe, 5 Mar 2020. A cheap low-difficulty side chain does not bloat the honest UTXO set if a block is rejected when its two immediate parents differ by more than a bound near `k` in blue score. A block dated in the future still has to wait for the clock. The follow-up is 21 Dec 2020. The thread does not cite itself as the live spam rule.

[62](https://research.kas.pa/t/including-transactions-of-red-blocks/62). Include transactions from red blocks so a user can see a fast first confirmation and so a temporary majority does not freeze ordinary payments. Fees are not paid to the red miner. hashdag’s 2024 reply: merge-set inclusion does not branch on color; the rewards of red blocks go to the merging block; blue score is what secures ordering; reds are still counted in the difficulty window, for a reason he does not finish in the post. The thread is the design conversation behind that behavior. It is not the KIP.

[63](https://research.kas.pa/t/node-sync-checks-for-mining-purposes/63). someone235, 20 Apr 2020. Bitcoin-style `getBlockTemplate` refuses to mine when the tip looks old (24 hours there, 12 hours in their node). A development network that stopped for a day never restarted, because every node thought it was unsynced. Trusting a peer’s version message is too cheap to fake. Their rule: serve a template unless the selected tip is older than X seconds and the peer has delivered a valid non-orphan block in the last Y minutes. A node with no peers does not serve templates. hashdag answers the next day that a small miner can still feed blocks at full rate and stop everyone else. He wants mining to stop only when the tip is old and the observed sync rate is above the expected block rate. He also notes that using the timestamp this way assumes difficulty adjustment has worked for the whole history. Topic 294 is the 2025 version of the same problem. Neither post is an operations manual for a live miner.

[64](https://research.kas.pa/t/on-finality-and-pow/64). hashdag, 21 Apr 2020. He is justifying a finality window of about 16 hours that the implementation of that month already used. Two corrections he wants on the record: weak subjectivity is not a proof-of-stake-only idea, and proof of work can have a finality rule. He treats Bitcoin checkpoints as a finality rule. The post does not enact today’s window. Today’s finality depth is whatever the shipped node uses, which this thread does not set.

[66](https://research.kas.pa/t/a-proposal-for-finality-in-ghostdag/66). hashdag, 3 May 2020, seventeen posts, the long finality argument. Finality means rejecting a block that would reorganize deeper than a depth M. Deshe finds a pruning attack against the first formula. Do not quote that first formula as the shipped pruning rule. The later intuition is topic 95 and the note it links, [Prunality.pdf](https://github.com/kaspanet/docs/blob/main/Reference/prunality/Prunality.pdf).

[68](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-consensus-category/68) and [69](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-finality-category/69). eyal, 11 May 2020. Consensus is for consensus rules and proposed improvements. Finality is for finality. Finality is a child of Consensus.

[85](https://research.kas.pa/t/dag-state-vs-consensus-state/85). elichai2, 21 Oct 2020, one post. He separates consensus state (the mutable UTXO set) from DAG state (append-only, and lazy: a block can sit at `UTXOPendingVerification` until it becomes `Valid` or `DisqualifiedFromChain`).

[87](https://research.kas.pa/t/switching-to-a-baw-based-daa/87). Deshe, 26 Oct 2020. Ranking and the difficulty window should use accumulated blue work. He writes that this scoring was already decided. That sentence means decided in the design, not “this forum post shipped it.” Blue score stays the clock for finality and pruning, because the difficulty algorithm is what targets blocks per second. The follow-up is topic 93.

[88](https://research.kas.pa/t/metrics-to-test-dag-performance/88). hashdag, one post. What to measure when the mempool is full: confirmation time, node resources (bandwidth, CPU, RAM, disk, storage), goodput, and reward non-linearity. He points at the inclusive paper, [inclusive_full.pdf](https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~yoni_sompo/pubs/15/inclusive_full.pdf), and he names elastic throughput. A measurement list, not a product spec.

[91](https://research.kas.pa/t/the-data-growth-of-kaspa/91). elichai2, while mainnet is still not out. A pruned node keeps headers, the UTXO set, and about three days of block data (he writes 244,838 of blue score). He estimates header growth around 4.8 to 14 GB per year, and an archive at a 100 KB block limit up to about 3 TB per year. He asks hashdag to show the header arithmetic and says the numbers can change. These are not current disk requirements.

[92](https://research.kas.pa/t/spv-mode-and-pruning/92). elichai2. SPV and the initial sync to a pruning point. The new pruning point’s selected chain has to contain the current pruning point and the finality point, or the operator is told. One post.

[93](https://research.kas.pa/t/difficulty-adjustment-and-time-measurement-in-dags/93). Nine posts, hashdag, Deshe, elichai2. The time that should govern minting and time-locks is the blocks the difficulty window actually used, not blue score and not past size. The thread does not close the question of whether pruning and finality should switch clocks. It links [zawy12/difficulty-algorithms](https://github.com/zawy12/difficulty-algorithms). A lower bound is enough for finality. An attacker under 50 percent can slow that clock by a bounded factor (on the order of `(1 − alpha) / 2` in the argument they give). Past size is a bad clock: cheap to inflate, and the speedup is bounded by the merge bound L.

[95](https://research.kas.pa/t/some-of-the-intuition-behind-the-design-of-the-invalidation-rules-for-pruning/95). Deshe, 17 Dec 2020, 3,984 views. Finality depth phi is smaller than pruning depth pi. A kosherizing block plus a bounded merge size L is the intuition that lets a node delete history without letting an attacker revive it. The formal note he points at is [Prunality.pdf](https://github.com/kaspanet/docs/blob/main/Reference/prunality/Prunality.pdf). This file did not re-fetch that PDF. The post is the intuition, not the activation.

[97](https://research.kas.pa/t/handling-timestamp-manipulations/97). Deshe, 21 Dec 2020. Timestamps are unreliable, from attackers and from clock drift, and the difficulty algorithm needs them. Too loose and difficulty can be pushed. Too strict and honest blocks are dropped. “Older” has to come from the same chronology as the difficulty algorithm, which at the time of the post is blue accumulated work (topic 87). A block too far in the past is invalid if its timestamp is earlier than the median of the last `2F − 1` blocks in its past: the miner already had that information. A block too far in the future (later than local time plus `F` times the target delay) is dropped and not permanently rejected, because the local clock is not in consensus. Against the difficulty algorithm of that month he works an example: a 10 percent manipulation bound, 120 seconds of drift, a 1 second target, which needs `F` at least 132 and a window `N` at least 2,640. Those are his example bounds for that algorithm. They are not a live parameter sheet.

[98](https://research.kas.pa/t/an-atlas-of-the-various-constants-and-dependencies-thereof/98). Deshe, 29 Dec 2020. An atlas of the constants as they stood in the design: maximum delay, the tolerance delta, block rate lambda (`defaultTargetTimePerBlock` is the delay), parent cap C (`defaultMaxBlockParents`), effective merge delay, blue anticone limit k (`defaultGHOSTDAGK`), merge-set limit L, finality depth phi, pruning depth pi. For delay 50, one block per second, and delta 0.05, the PHANTOM paper’s subsection 4.2 gives k = 18, and he says to use the effective delay rather than the raw delay if those two are not equal. He is explicit that some of these exist in the code and some exist only for the argument. This is a December 2020 map. It is not today’s parameter file. Crescendo’s 10 blocks per second came years later, through KIP-14, not through this post.

## 2021–2022 — storage, coinbase dust, pruning arithmetic

[106](https://research.kas.pa/t/visualization-of-dags/106). hashdag, 24 Feb 2021. A wish list: a configurable DAG visualizer, a richer testnet transaction generator, rough CPU and disk estimates, a step-through of longest chain, Inclusive-GHOST, SPECTRE, and PHANTOM, and a dashboard of time-to-inclusion and of how often a small miner finds a block. He says a few large payloads (he says “10, say” transactions per second) would keep a full node more reasonable than many tiny ones, and he mentions rollups as the community need he has in mind. Wishes. No tool shipped from the post.

[117](https://research.kas.pa/t/updated-survey-of-the-data-growth-in-kaspad/117). Deshe, 1 Dec 2021, after launch. Three things stored: full headers above the pruning block, the UTXO set at the pruning block, and a proof of that set. At a 100 KB payload and one block per second, three days of full blocks is about 26 GB, assuming every block is full. Headers are bounded near 1.5 KB under a log-of-past-size assumption he says holds for about 30,000 years at one block per second. The correctness proof adds a negligible number of extra headers. He says the code still kept all headers because removing them safely had not been done yet, and that pruning-block headers add about 1.5 KB per day. The UTXO set is the part that grows with users. He intends cryptographic accumulators later so a node stores a commitment and users store proofs of their own outputs. The post does not ship accumulators. The 2020 disk figures and these 2021 figures are both historical.

[122](https://research.kas.pa/t/miner-funds-consolidation/122). mikez, 3 Jan 2022, seven posts. Every mined block creates one new coinbase UTXO. That bloats the set and makes the coins awkward to spend, and a ten-times block rate would mint ten times the outputs. Affiele suggests a compounding transaction that pays a bonus for merging many coinbases. msutton rejects the bonus: it changes total emission and pays larger miners more. Allowing a coinbase to spend an earlier UTXO needs a signature, or, without a signature, opens replay and spending on someone else’s behalf. ey51 suggests a miner flag that rolls one or n “snowball” outputs and signs when a block is found. The last reply, in December 2022, is a thank-you with no design. The later answer to this pressure is the additive-address work in topic 168 and KIP-10. This thread does not enact it.

[127](https://research.kas.pa/t/pruning-point-heuristic-formula/127). msutton, 3 Mar 2022. A note, posted for the formulas. Finality score is blue score divided by finality depth, floored. The pruning point moves only when a candidate has a higher finality score than the selected parent’s pruning point, so it steps by finality depth. He claims the codebase heuristic at [kaspad `ce4f5fcc`](https://github.com/kaspanet/kaspad/blob/ce4f5fcc33bf985bd78b110170b15ce6d74d5f83/domain/consensus/processes/pruningmanager/pruningmanager.go#L998) is not equivalent, because of that floor: the code can skip a search it should have done. He asks whether fixing it breaks consensus. The thread does not record a fix. He also says the live pruning point was already non-sliding relative to the [Prunality](https://github.com/kaspanet/docs/blob/main/Reference/prunality/Prunality.pdf) writeup.

## 2024 — mass, addresses, then the L2 frame

[159](https://research.kas.pa/t/quadratic-storage-mass-and-kip9/159). Deshe2, 29 Mar 2024, eleven posts. The discussion house for KIP-9: quadratic storage mass, local constraints against UTXO growth. The KIP file is the law. KIP-9 is Active on the Now board. The thread also holds practical questions (a merchant who does not want an arbitrary payer adding to a hot output). Those questions are not extra consensus rules.

[168](https://research.kas.pa/t/auto-compounding-additive-addresses-kip10-draft/168). michaelsutton, 3 Apr 2024, twenty-one posts, 2,421 views. The draft that became KIP-10. A payer may spend a recipient output without the owner’s signature if the same transaction creates a larger output to the same script. It has to be opt-in: if every output worked this way, a stranger could outrace the owner by adding a negligible amount. A minimum increment prices that spam. A second idea is a semi-signature shared with trusted senders. Encoding is either a new script version with the threshold in the prefix, which wallets can scan, or a P2SH of the ordinary script plus the threshold. The script needs an opcode that reads an output’s amount and script. He also sketches additive mining, so a coinbase can roll the previous reward into one output, which matters if the chain goes to 10 blocks per second and mints on the order of a million rewards a day. The replies argue owner priority, a reuse counter, griefing (coderofstuff’s example is one huge output and a tiny threshold, chained for years at 1 block per second), storage-mass bounds (Deshe2 shows the attacker’s own budget still has to pay), and introspection opcodes. biryukovmaxim says one counter-and-limit script is in a [rusty-kaspa commit](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/pull/487/commits/e5e8b64ef4d0fa31ad1f080ef6e5f7e6e775bf93). The opening calls itself a draft before a well-formed KIP. KIP-10 is Active. This thread is not the activation.

[189](https://research.kas.pa/t/kip-6-discussion-thread/189). someone235, 7 Oct 2024, four posts. A discussion thread for the existing [KIP-6](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0006.md) document. He finds the pruning-point formula awkward (finality depth, plus twice the merge-depth bound, plus terms in the mergeset limit and `k`) and suggests replacing it with `P = 3F` (72 hours), storing previous posterity instead of the pruning point, and optionally a one-hour posterity period. He also suggests a Merkle mountain range in place of the proof-of-chain-membership root, linking the [OpenTimestamps description](https://github.com/opentimestamps/opentimestamps-server/blob/master/doc/merkle-mountain-range.md). Those are proposals inside the thread. KIP-6 is still Draft on the Now board.

[193](https://research.kas.pa/t/atomic-composability-and-other-considerations-for-l1-l2-support/193). hashdag, 12 Nov 2024, one post, 6,674 views, the most-viewed topic. From talks with Sutton, Roma, and Ilia at StarkWare. L2 activity is stored on L1 as payloads. Each logic zone’s state is committed in headers. He uses “logic zone,” “smart contract,” and “rollup” as the same word on purpose. Based rollups are the ones sequenced on the L1. A prover anchors a new state with a zero-knowledge proof checked by a new opcode. Proofs may be late. A node that follows the zone can still confirm in about 100 ms. Proof delay is not finality delay. It changes how a new node syncs, how long L1 must keep data, and what a cross-zone call costs. He wants one transaction to call another zone and read and write it in that same transaction. He says early Ethereum had this and that the rollup roadmap replaced it with asynchronous bridge messages. Based sequencing keeps the atomic case because the data is on chain until the pruning point. He does not want that to collapse into one global program: a zone runs another zone’s code only when they interact, execution is far cheaper than proving, and provers need not be commodity hardware for the whole system. He urges L2 projects to keep zones small. He also wants mass and gas capped separately, which makes block building a two-dimensional knapsack. He notes they had already folded script-computation mass and KIP-9 storage mass into one mass function and accepted that coupling. Parts of the post are marked work in progress. No reply. Not shipped.

[206](https://research.kas.pa/t/about-the-l1-l2-category/206). michaelsutton, 2 Dec 2024. The category is for Kaspa’s L1-to-L2 bridge design.

[208](https://research.kas.pa/t/on-the-design-of-based-zk-rollups-over-kaspas-utxo-based-dag-consensus/208). michaelsutton, 9 Dec 2024, nine posts, 4,236 views. He calls it a non-final picture, and he points back at topic 193 plus the Ethereum based-rollup and zk-rollup notes. User data sits on L1 in L1 order, so the L2 cannot censor or reorder it. Provers submit succinct proofs so L1 does not re-execute. L1 aggregates those blobs in arrival order, checks proofs, updates a stored commitment, and has to support deposits and withdrawals of KAS. On a UTXO chain the minimum design is one rollup and one state output. A block’s mergeset is its past minus the past of its GHOSTDAG selected parent. A new header field, an ordered-history Merkle root, commits that order. `OpZkVerify` checks a proof. `OpChainBlockHistoryRoot` returns the root of an earlier chain block and fails if that block is too deep or is not a chain block. The state output is a P2SH of the program hash, the L2 commitment, the history root, verification data, and the script. A proof transaction spends it and creates the next one. Open points he lists: which output is the authorized commitment, deposits and withdrawals that can exceed a transaction’s output limit, many rollups without forcing long-term per-rollup storage, and several commitments per rollup. Replies ask about exits, reorgs near the tip, and whether an operator makes the design non-based. his answer on reorgs: if the anchor disappears, the history-root opcode fails, the proof transaction is invalid, and provers resubmit for the new order. someone235 warns that a history-root opcode makes script validity depend on more than the transaction and its inputs, so a spend can die without a deliberate double spend. The author says this is not a final design. Not shipped.

[219](https://research.kas.pa/t/additional-practical-considerations-re-hash-function-and-zk-opcodes/219). reshmem, 12 Dec 2024, twenty-three posts. Not a full design. Change L1 as little as possible, support many L2s, and bound the cost of proof checks on every node. He proposes Groth16 or Plonk because the proofs are small and verification is constant time, on BN254, with three script opcodes: field addition, multiplication, and a pairing check. Two 256-bit public inputs cover almost any logic. For the history tree he wants a hash that is fast on ordinary CPUs and friendly to proving. He says Blake2s and Blake3 are CPU-friendly and at least about twenty times more expensive to prove than Poseidon2, and Poseidon2 depends on a field. proof’s reply: Blake is cheap to execute and not zk-native; Poseidon is the one he would pick for a small field; the decision needs benchmarks. hashdag leans toward L1 execution over prover convenience, so Poseidon unless new arguments appear, and he wants the safety question answered. FreshAir08 asks whether the opcodes still allow STARKs with no trusted setup. reshmem’s answer is a two-stage chain: a STARK or a zkVM proves the computation, Groth16 or Plonk proves that the verifier accepted, and L1 checks only the outer proof. He says a STARK verifier itself does not fit this script, and that they are implementing the curve opcodes so a later system does not by itself force another hard fork. He also says a hash-based proof still goes through that outer SNARK in this construction. superMainnet argues for Plonk over Groth16 and against a hard-fork culture. The opening says this is not a detailed design. KIP-16, which grew out of this area, is Active on the Now board. The thread is not the activation. reshmem’s Groth16 sizes (a 192-byte proof, a verification key on the order of 640 bytes for a short public input) are examples in the post, not live script limits.

[237](https://research.kas.pa/t/updateable-list-of-l1-l2-topics-to-flesh-out-before-finalizing-design/237). hashdag, 23 Dec 2024. A checklist of what still has to be written before an initial L1-to-L2 design. He marks the high-level opcodes (topic 208) and the low-level opcodes (topic 219) as published. Later edits of the same post mark uniqueness, conflicting proofs, sequencing commitments, the bridge, and fees as written, and leave multiple commitments per rollup, and synchronous composability, unmarked. A checklist. Not a design, and not shipped.

## 2025 — bridge, Crescendo’s discussion, then vProgs

[247](https://research.kas.pa/t/fees-and-throughput-regulation-dynamics/247). hashdag, 7 Jan 2025. Permissionless L2s need a throughput cap, because gas is load on every operator and provers are two or three orders of magnitude heavier than executors. If the cap lives only inside the L2 program, L1 can include transactions the L2 ignores. He wants the issuer to declare a maximum gas, consensus to reject a block with more than X gas for one L2, and a transaction over its own declaration to revert and still pay. Unused declared gas is still paid, from L1’s point of view. High demand then pays sequencers rather than provers. He prefers protecting L1’s security budget. A per-block gas limit makes block building a multidimensional knapsack, which he says is already the shape of KIP-9 storage mass, and he points at KIP-13. someone235’s alternative is to pay a fraction of the L1 fees to the L2’s script. FreshAir08 would lock an inner fee until a proof arrives, and pay more for proofs that arrive on time. Not shipped.

[251](https://research.kas.pa/t/conflicting-proofs-policy/251). FreshAir08, 8 Jan 2025. This is the topic the old catalog skipped. Two provers can publish different proofs of the same settlement. If the shorter one lands first, a naive double-spend rule drops the longer one, which wastes work and lets a liveness attacker settle one block at a time. One fix accepts a more inclusive proof after the old settlement point is obsolete. The other accepts the loss if proofs can be recombined from stages. reshmem, 24 Jan 2025: recursion and folding are system-specific. He wants L1 to ignore the policy for the next one to two years and let each L2 choose. Not a consensus change.

[258](https://research.kas.pa/t/l1-l2-canonical-bridge-entry-exit-mechanism/258). michaelsutton, 13 Jan 2025. A canonical bridge. Bridged KAS is 1:1 with L1 KAS. Entry pays a well-known L2 address and names the L2 destination in the payload, and L1 can check that immediately. Exit needs an authorization L1 cannot check except by a proof. The attack he works through: anyone can pay a script that copies the canonical text but carries a forged state, then pull real deposits into a separate chain. His answer hardcodes a genesis output in the program and checks, inside the proof, that each state output’s parent is the previous proof, back to that genesis. A later note says a StarkWare OP_CAT bridge covenant uses a similar two-step lookback. Exits can overflow a block’s mass. One remedy spreads withdrawal rights through a Merkle tree. The other refuses to advance the state until an outbox is cleared. Not a live bridge.

[267](https://research.kas.pa/t/optimal-transaction-selection-a-multidimensional-knapsack-problem/267). coderofstuff, 18 Jan 2025, one post, no reply. He treats KIP-9 persistent storage mass and KIP-13 transient storage mass, plus compute mass, as already specified, each checked against its own limit, with a block mass limit of 500,000 grams. The open question is how a mempool should pick a template in that multidimensional space. He does not give an algorithm.

[274](https://research.kas.pa/t/subnets-sequencing-commitments/274). FreshAir08, 20 Jan 2025. A sequencing commitment so a rollup proves only its own transactions. If every transaction is in one hash, a rollup that is active once every five minutes still walks the whole chain. His proposal, credited to michaelsutton, puts a subnet id on each transaction and keeps a per-subnet history inside the commitment. A subnet that goes quiet past finality depth can be dropped from the active tree. The fallback hash still covers every transaction, so a pruned subnet can prove without the optimization, and cross-subnet order can be kept by storing each transaction’s index. michaelsutton recaps the stored tree as bounded by unexpired subnets, with a proof linear in the subnet’s own activity if it transacts at least once per finality epoch. Not shipped.

[279](https://research.kas.pa/t/crescendo-hardfork-discussion-thread/279). michaelsutton, 21 Jan 2025. The discussion thread for KIP-14, the 10-block-per-second Crescendo hard fork. someone235 quotes the KIP: the pruning-depth formula is a lower bound, 627,258 blocks, about 17.42 hours, and the KIP suggests rounding toward 30 hours. He prefers an integer multiple of finality depth. michaelsutton says 36 hours raises the transient-storage upper bound from 190 GB to about 230 GB and that this still looks reasonable. reshmem asks that KIP-15 ride in the same hard fork. On 5 Mar 2025 he says KIP-15 is being tested. Both KIP-14 and KIP-15 are Active on the Now board. “Being tested” in this thread is not the activation. Crescendo at about 10 blocks per second is on the Live row.

[293](https://research.kas.pa/t/thoughts-about-covenant-and-async-message-standardization/293). michaelsutton, 31 Jan 2025, one post. He wants the bridge’s covenant-chain check standardized so L1 can name an identity. A script header would mask the static bytes (the canonical script and the program) and hash them into a covenant id. A proof transaction could then carry a payload whose sender id must equal the covenant id of a spent input. The same id would simplify the delegation script in topic 258. No reply. Not shipped.

[294](https://research.kas.pa/t/sync-monitor-mining-rule-engine/294). coderofstuff, 1 Feb 2025, one post. A sync monitor answers whether to mine, including during a halt. A rule engine answers how. He says rusty-kaspa mines when there is at least one peer and the sink is recent, which cannot restart a chain that has been quiet long enough for every sink to look old. Sync rate is observed blocks per second over the expected rate (1, soon 10). Below a threshold he does not set, mine anyway. If headers are slow and red blocks keep appearing, mine only on blue. If the failure is the BadMerkleRoot class from the [20-minute freeze post-mortem](https://someone235.medium.com/kaspa-20-minute-blockdag-freeze-post-mortem-7f58bc309bd6/), mine empty blocks. The post does not say these rules shipped.

[295](https://research.kas.pa/t/a-proposal-towards-elastic-throughput/295). hashdag, 5 Feb 2025. A proposal for prepaid transient storage. A commodity node needs a hard cap. He describes a 200 GB cap per pruning epoch of about 42 hours, split evenly across the blocks of the epoch, so unused room in one block cannot move to another and a transaction larger than one block’s share is rejected even when the epoch is not full. A miner who under-fills early blocks would earn credit and spend it on a later block, proved by a signature that the same miner made the earlier block. Credits zero at the pruning boundary. He still wants a hard per-block cap (his example is 2 MB) because large blocks widen the DAG. He says the same idea might apply to persistent storage mass, and that compute-mass credit should be left aside because peak CPU is what matters. The point of the large-block case is native STARK proofs of a few hundred kilobytes. coderofstuff asks how a credit that never expires can bound persistent storage, which KIP-9 treats as ongoing UTXO growth. This post does not raise the live block rate. The Now board’s “no 100 BPS” stands. Reading the post as a live rate is the mistake that chip already marks.

[303](https://research.kas.pa/t/kip-15-discussion-thread/303). someone235, 23 Feb 2025, one post. A pointer at [kip-0015.md](https://github.com/kaspanet/kips/blob/master/kip-0015.md). The post does not describe the KIP. KIP-15 is Active. The thread does not enact it.

[323](https://research.kas.pa/t/a-basic-framework-for-proofs-stitching/323). FreshAir08, 2 Apr 2025. A proof of concept for atomic synchronous composability. One transaction calls several zones. Proving the whole transaction as one program would collapse the zones. Each prover proves its own segment, and the proofs are stitched. He likens the idea to LegoSNARK ([eprint 2019/142](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/142)) and treats the SNARK as a black box. A proof can be internally valid and still not be on the real trace, so it stays conditional until the stitch reaches the end. Hans_Moog says each input needs a commitment that it came from the previous output, and that settling partial proofs on L1 before the stitch forces a rollback to be proved as well. FreshAir08’s later reply prefers independent execution over a trusted prover network, and he does not want the settled root to move until stitching is done, so there is nothing to roll back. An initial proof of concept. Not shipped.

[346](https://research.kas.pa/t/kip-draft-threshold-address-format-for-kip-10-mutual-transactions/346). biryukovmaxim, 22 May 2025. A draft address for KIP-10 mutual spending, aimed at compounding and mining-pool outputs, which he says needs no consensus change. Version byte 9. A 35-byte payload: a 32-byte X coordinate plus a 3-byte float (signature type, Y parity, 17 mantissa bits, 5 exponent bits). Threshold is mantissa times two to the exponent. The maximum he computes is about 281 trillion sompi, which he says does not reach a 29 billion KAS supply. The script is: the key signs, or the output script matches and the amount grew by at least the threshold. A second post notes that address-to-script conversion is one way. The title says KIP draft. It is not an Active KIP. There is no KIP-26, or any other number, on the master KIP list for this text.

[347](https://research.kas.pa/t/on-the-inherent-tension-between-multileader-consensus-and-inclusion-time-proving/347). michaelsutton, 22 May 2025. A parallel L1 includes transactions before their final order. He treats that as a feature (the producer does not know the sequence). Inclusion-time proving of a cross-zone call wants the pre-state at the moment of inclusion, which that parallelism does not provide. He chooses the multileader consensus, so proofs wait until ordering defines a state. His rule is timebound proof settlement: data is on L1 first, and cross-domain effects settle only if proofs arrive within a window T. A missing proof fails that segment. Censoring a prover does not block L1 inclusion. He says proving is moving toward well under 12 seconds. Pavel_Emdin suggests a default around five minutes. FreshAir08 wants complex, unlikely proofs to get less time, not more, and wants the timeouts globally known. Not shipped. Topic 387 later says this window is unnecessary if synchronous confirmation really tracks L1 sequencing.

[375](https://research.kas.pa/t/data-availability-concerns/375). Hans_Moog, 11 Jul 2025. A problem statement, with a proposal promised later. If execution moves to zero-knowledge rollups and L1 prunes, the latest rollup state depends on whoever kept it. Withholding can lock funds. Voluntary replication is a commons: a rational actor stores only what affects them. His attack is an untracked zone whose history vanishes, then composed into someone else’s state, so only the attacker can advance the shared commitment. hashdag answers that replication is voluntary on an ordinary chain too, that refusing to serve data is the same class of failure as a miner refusing the UTXO set to a new node, and that he would rather the platform be rollup-unfriendly. He suggests retiring “logic zone” for “vApp.” YesComrade compares Igra, Kasplex, and Sparkle, and treats KRC-20 as the most “based” design he sees because it creates no blocks of its own. Pavel_Emdin answers for Igra: a modified Reth, standard Ethereum RPC so users stay on MetaMask, a temporary 20 kB payload limit, gas paid in iKAS from a canonical bridge backed 1:1 by KAS locked on L1. Those are implementers describing their own systems. No data-availability rule became protocol in this thread.

[387](https://research.kas.pa/t/concrete-proposal-for-a-synchronously-composable-verifiable-programs-architecture/387). hashdag with michaelsutton, 7 Aug 2025, four posts, 4,103 views. The concrete vProgs proposal. Accounts hold state. A vProg owns accounts and defines their transitions. Transactions declare reads and writes in advance. Each vProg is a small zkVM with its own L1 covenant and its own gas scale. Another vProg does not trust its execution or its state availability. Synchronous composability lets one transaction read one account and write another, atomically, while each program keeps sole ownership. He says that without this, liquidity moves into rollups that compose inside themselves and then will not interoperate. Dependency is the touched account, not the whole program. Still under research: which features need a standard proof that the covenant locks a conforming program (he calls that a zk-SBOM). If synchronous confirmation really tracks L1 sequencing, the timebound window of topic 347 is unnecessary. Scope is the transitions back to the latest witness already anchored by a proof. Proofs publish state to L1 and stop scopes from cascading. Each vProg has its own provers, who may also prove another program’s segment. He sets a yellow-paper draft for the end of that month (August 2025) and asks developers to say what they can contribute. oudeis says Igra was aligned and will contribute. Two later questions, about the largest practical problem and about lost witnesses, have no answer in the thread. This is the architecture post. It is not a product, and it is not a public testnet. The Now board already records Sutton’s later statement that Max’s demo is not full yellow-paper synchronous composability.

[407](https://research.kas.pa/t/zoom-in-a-formal-backbone-model-for-the-vprog-computation-dag/407). michaelsutton, 17 Aug 2025. A formal model of the cost of synchronous composability on one sequencer. A transaction has a declared read set, a write set contained in the read set, and witnesses that cut scope. A proof commits a Merkle root over the steps since the previous proof. Scope is found by walking backward from the reads and stopping at anchors. He first defines scope from the past set, then corrects it: a program’s past contains vertices it never stored, so the past relation is the wrong definition. The routable definition replaces it. With proofs every F transactions, an Erdős–Rényi sketch says a giant component appears when F is larger than about N/q, and below that the extra work is logarithmic. FreshAir08 adds a gas sketch that prices scope from declared proof-gas, and he warns that refunding unused gas lets a rarely written program inflate the next reader’s bill. A model and a correction of that model. Not shipped.

[410](https://research.kas.pa/t/on-transaction-scopes-and-the-visibility-of-the-object-dag/410). hashdag, 20 Aug 2025, one post. A transaction that forces work without paying for it is a sovereignty bug. He wants declared reads and writes to always create the vertices the topology promises, including a failed write, which copies the previous value. An oracle that overwrites without reading would, under a forced read, inflate scope by every write in the proof epoch. He rejects “oracles are rare” as a shortcut and sketches an optional sequence index so a write-only transaction can name which write it is. He is leaning toward putting the object DAG in L1 consensus: nodes track declarations and gas commitments, and they stay ignorant of execution. He defines a vProg as the stateless logic. The design follows Solana and Sui in static declarations and rejects Aptos-style dynamic detection, because scopes would then require executing every program. Not shipped.

[411](https://research.kas.pa/t/pruning-safety-in-the-vprogs-architecture/411). FreshAir08, 25 Aug 2025. If L1 can see the computation DAG, a scope that needs data below the pruning point must be forbidden, or success would depend on which node still has the bytes. The substitute is a witness of a later account proof inside the pruning window. Vertices far behind a new proof can be dropped. His example delays are 500 rounds and 50 rounds, and he says both are placeholders. Gordon_Murray restates the rule with four examples and does not change the numbers. Not shipped.

[429](https://research.kas.pa/t/random-linear-network-coding-for-scalable-blockdag/429). Gordon_Murray, 21 Sep 2025, six posts. An independent proposal for random linear network coding on the peer-to-peer layer, including wireless mesh. He writes that Kaspa is at about 10 blocks per second and aims above 100. That aim is his. It is not a live rate, and the Now board’s “no 100 BPS” is unchanged. He places the coding only in the network layer: mining and GHOSTDAG stay as they are. His sketch uses GF(256) shards, and he claims on the order of 40 to 60 percent less bandwidth in a simulation (20 Mbps, 5 peers, 20 percent loss). FreshAir08 says the parent list is not one to three hashes: indirect parents are often several hundred, and that list is a large part of the redundancy. The later reply limits itself to transaction bytes: overlapping transactions become one source item, peers are not asked for specific transactions, and dependent shards are dropped. biryukovmaxim asks about a denial of service from shards that never increase rank. The rollout he asks for is an opt-in flag after simpa tests. He links a [demo repository](https://github.com/gordonmurray-coding/rlnc-kaspa-ibd-demo). Not shipped. Not a consensus change.
